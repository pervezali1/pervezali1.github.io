Research

I study how Langevin dynamics can be designed and analysed to efficiently sample from complex distributions. My current work falls under the first two themes below; the third is a direction I want to develop.

  • Current research

    Langevin dynamics and nonreversible sampling

    When does adding a nonreversible drift make a Langevin sampler converge faster, and how can the gain be measured?

    Overdamped Langevin dynamics is the starting point for many sampling algorithms. Adding a skew-symmetric drift leaves the target distribution unchanged but changes how the process moves through it. I study when this helps, using large deviations and nonasymptotic convergence bounds.

  • Current research

    Constrained Sampling with Penalized Nonreversible Langevin Algorithms

    How can we combine penalization and nonreversible Langevin dynamics to efficiently sample from probability distributions supported on constrained sets?

    Many models in statistics and machine learning restrict parameters to compact convex sets. We develop Langevin sampling algorithms that combine squared-distance penalties with nonreversible dynamics. Our research establishes convergence and accuracy guarantees for full-gradient and stochastic-gradient methods, accounting for both numerical discretization and the approximation introduced by penalization. We also investigate when nonreversibility can accelerate convergence, combining theoretical analysis with numerical experiments on constrained sampling problems.

  • Future direction

    Sampling Theory for Generative Modeling

    How can we generate samples more accurately and at lower computational cost?

    Building on my research in Langevin dynamics, I plan to investigate how numerical discretization and the choice of sampling dynamics affect convergence and approximation error in diffusion-based generative models. I aim to combine theoretical analysis with numerical experiments to develop sampling algorithms that achieve a desired accuracy with less computation.

Selected projects

2026with Weihao Dong and Xiaoyu Wang

Penalized nonreversible Langevin for constrained sampling

Sampling on a compact convex set by combining a squared-distance penalty with a skew-symmetric perturbation of Langevin dynamics. The preprint gives nonasymptotic bounds relative to the penalized target: in total variation with full gradients, including nonconvex potentials, and in 2-Wasserstein distance with stochastic gradients. For a quadratic model, tuning the perturbation improves the sufficient number of iterations from linear to logarithmic in the curvature ratio.

  • Preprint (arXiv:2609.25381)
  • Code

2025with Nian Yao, Xihua Tao and Lingjiong Zhu

Accelerating Langevin Monte Carlo sampling: a large deviations analysis

Large deviation principles with explicit rate functions for mirror, high-order and Hessian-free high-resolution Langevin dynamics. Comparing rate functions shows when these dynamics, and underdamped Langevin, accelerate overdamped Langevin. Experiments cover Bayesian linear and logistic regression, including real data.

About

Pervez Ali is a PhD candidate in the Department of Mathematics at Florida State University, advised by Dr. Lingjiong Zhu, and a Fulbright Scholar. His research is on sampling algorithms based on stochastic dynamics, including Langevin methods, nonreversible dynamics and constrained sampling.

He is a Lecturer in Mathematics at Sukkur IBA University in Pakistan, where he taught from 2017 to 2022 and also completed an MS in Mathematics. He has been on study leave from Sukkur IBA since July 2022. At Florida State he has served as vice president of the SIAM Student Chapter.
